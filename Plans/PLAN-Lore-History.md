# PLAN - Lore & History

**Status:** Design draft, 2026-04-17 (Tuvok)
**Owner:** Captain (LostBeard)
**Audience:** Narrative designers, quest writers, world-builders, AI agents writing content

---

## Purpose

This plan establishes the canonical backstory of Lost Spawns' world. It is the source of truth that every holotape, NPC dialog line, found document, terminal log, and environmental story must trace back to. If a piece of lore in the shipped game contradicts this plan, either this plan is updated (with Captain's sign-off) or the lore is wrong.

This is the companion to [PLAN-Quests-Storyline.md](PLAN-Quests-Storyline.md), which defines **how** story is delivered. This plan defines **what** the story is.

## The Pitch

Eleven months ago the world died. Not all at once. In pieces, over forty-seven days. By the time the power went out in the last major city, the people who knew what was happening had already stopped talking, and the people still talking did not know what was happening. You, the player, were in a cryo-shelter. You woke up ninety days later than scheduled, into a world that has moved on without you, and the only voice on the radio calls itself Aether Group.

This plan tells the writers exactly what happened, who did it, why, and what is still happening. Players may NEVER learn all of this. Most will piece together a fragment. That is the point. But the writers have to know the whole picture so that the fragments fit together.

## Design principles

### [COMMIT] Lore is discovered, never delivered

A player should never sit through a cutscene explaining what The Cascade was. Every piece of truth is on a holotape, in a terminal, on a corpse's pre-Cascade ID badge, painted on a wall, carved into a cryo-pod lid. The world tells the story in fragments and the player assembles it.

**Why:** Fallout 76's best moments are standing in a dead town reading a recipe card and realizing this used to be a family. Lost Spawns aims for that texture. Cutscenes break it.

### [COMMIT] Multiple conflicting accounts of every key event

For every major lore point, at least three in-world sources exist and at least one of them is wrong. A holotape from a dying scientist says one thing. A Faithful preacher's sermon says another. An Aether Group broadcast says a third. The **canonical truth** in this document is knowable by a player who chases all the fragments, but no single source delivers it clean.

**Why:** Real-world post-apocalypse survivors would disagree about what happened. Propaganda, faith, denial, partial information, and active deception all shape what people believe. The player's job is to be the investigator.

### [COMMIT] The world has a real history, not a setting

Every named faction has an origin that predates the Cascade, or a clean reason for forming after it. Every landmark has a pre-Cascade identity (school, clinic, pharmaceutical plant, radio station) with environmental storytelling that sells it. Every key NPC has a past they can talk about if asked.

### [LIKELY] Some truths are permanently unknowable

Not every mystery has a canonical answer. A handful of things are genuinely unknown (who fired the first shot in the Aether-Warden skirmish of day 31? what was the weather in the Burn Scar region the day the bombs fell?) and should stay that way. Writers can refuse to answer.

**Why:** A world where every question has an answer is a world with no mystery. Keep some ambiguity deliberate.

### [COMMIT] No retcons after ship

Once this plan ships to v1.0, the canonical truths in it are locked. DLC/expansions can add NEW information that recontextualizes old lore, but cannot contradict it. A holotape found in the base game stays true forever.

## The Cascade (the canonical truth)

### [COMMIT] What it was

The Cascade was a **man-made bioengineered pathogen outbreak** that escaped containment, cascaded into four distinct secondary effects, and collapsed civilization over a forty-seven-day window. The pathogen itself was engineered as a weapon. The cascading effects were not planned and are the reason the world died instead of just losing a country.

The four cascades, in order:

1. **Cascade 1 (days 1-11): The Pathogen.** A respiratory-transmissible agent designated internally as **Project SERAPH-3**. High transmissibility, moderate fatality, and a documented neurological progression in survivors: aggression, sensitivity to sound, diminished higher cognition. These are the **Infected** of PLAN-Infected-AI.md. Not every infected person died, and not everyone who survived came back as a full person.

2. **Cascade 2 (days 8-24): The Mutagenic Tail.** A small percentage of infected underwent a second, slower, genetic-scale mutation that produced what the world now calls **cryptids**. This was not planned. The engineers of SERAPH-3 did not know the pathogen contained a retroviral element that would stitch foreign DNA into the survivor's genome over weeks. The cryptids of PLAN-Dynamic-World-Events.md are the result.

3. **Cascade 3 (days 14-31): The Networks.** As the pathogen spread, governments and corporations activated emergency protocols that were never designed to run concurrently: internet throttling, automated financial market freezes, power-grid load-shedding. These protocols fed on each other. The global financial system collapsed in a thirty-hour window on day 19. Power grids went regional or went dark. The internet fragmented into national intranets, then into nothing.

4. **Cascade 4 (days 28-47): The Response.** The military and paramilitary response to Cascades 1-3 was itself a catastrophe. Quarantine zones were bombed to contain cryptid breakouts. Cities were firebombed as sterilization measures. Entire populations were relocated, abandoned, or "processed" (the Aether Group term, which still appears in their broadcasts). By day 47, coordinated human civilization no longer functioned at a scale larger than a city district.

### [COMMIT] Who did it

**Project SERAPH-3 was engineered at a joint military-corporate research facility known as Hilltop Station** (see PLAN-World-Biomes-Regions.md, landmark "Aether Hilltop Station"). The facility was operated under a cover identity as a pharmaceutical research park. Its actual funding source was a classified defense consortium spanning three countries. The project was in its eighth year when containment failed.

**The containment failure was not an accident.** It was caused by a researcher named **Dr. Mara Helvetia** who believed the project was going to be deployed against a civilian population in a specific conflict and leaked the strain to what she thought was an international press contact. The contact was compromised. The strain left the facility in an unsecured vial. Helvetia is almost certainly dead. Her story is told in three separate holotapes scattered across Hilltop Station, and the player who finds all three will understand that the world ended because one person tried to stop a worse thing, and failed.

### [LIKELY] What the in-world accounts say

Canonical truth above. In-world, three major accounts circulate:

- **The Aether Group account (broadcast regularly):** "An unprecedented natural pandemic combined with environmental factors caused the Cascade. Aether Group was the first to coordinate a response. Aether Group is building civilization back. Report to the nearest Aether Group outpost for processing." This is a deliberate lie that obscures Aether Group's own role.
- **The Faithful account (sermons, pamphlets, painted on walls):** "The Cascade was divine judgment on a world that had grown corrupt. The Infected are the unworthy. The cryptids are angels of wrath. The only safety is in faith and in cleansing the world of its rot." This is a reconstruction by survivors who needed a narrative.
- **The Carrion account (campfire stories, bandit-king monologues):** "Someone did this to us on purpose. The government. A corporation. An enemy nation. It doesn't matter who. What matters is we're free now and the strong take what they want." This is the closest to the truth but has none of the specifics.

The Mothfolk have a fourth account, but it is communicated through ritual and not words, and its content is given in the cryptid-origin section below.

### [UNDECIDED] Is The Cascade still happening?

Two possibilities for late-game reveal:

- **A) The Cascade is over.** What's happening now is the long tail, not a new event.
- **B) The Cascade is ongoing.** Cascade 2 (mutagenic tail) is still active. New cryptids are still being born. A Cascade 5 is possible.

**Recommendation: B**, because it gives the late-game arc a real stake. The player can believe they're the person who prevents Cascade 5, or the person who witnesses it, or the person who causes it by making the wrong choice. Captain's call.

## Pre-Cascade world state

### [COMMIT] What the world was like eleven months ago

Broadly: 2020s-2030s tech level. Smartphones common but not ubiquitous. Electric vehicles present but rare. A working financial system, working internet, working governments. The world was recognizably our world plus ten to fifteen years of drift.

**Politically:** Tensions between three major power blocs that had been cold-warring for roughly a decade. Proxy conflicts in three regions. An active arms race in biological, cyber, and orbital domains. Project SERAPH-3 was part of the biological leg.

**Technologically:** Mature AI-assistant tools in consumer devices (cross-ref Aether Group's automated broadcast infrastructure, which still runs on pre-Cascade AI). Robust satellite network (some of which is still functional and is a quest hook). Advanced but not post-scarcity materials science. No practical fusion. No meaningful space colonization.

**Culturally:** A decade into a rise in distrust of institutions. The Faithful's religious fervor has pre-Cascade roots; their founder was a preacher whose pre-Cascade podcast is still discoverable on old phones. The Carrion's ideology traces to pre-Cascade militia movements. The Mothfolk have no pre-Cascade equivalent; they are wholly a post-Cascade emergence.

### [COMMIT] The peninsula setting (cross-ref PLAN-World-Biomes-Regions.md)

The game takes place on a peninsula roughly 50 km^2. Before the Cascade it was:

- A mixed rural/suburban region with three small cities (Old Port, Mercy, Coalton), several villages, and significant industrial infrastructure (Hilltop Station, Mercy Industrial Park, Coalton Refinery)
- Economic anchors: pharmaceutical research (Hilltop), port trade (Old Port), light industry (Mercy), fossil-fuel processing (Coalton)
- Notable: a military base (Warden Garrison) at the peninsula's neck, a major hospital (Old Mercy Hospital) in Mercy, a civilian airport and a small university campus in Old Port
- Population: approximately 180,000 peninsula-wide, skewing older (retiree coastal communities) and lower-income (inland)

The peninsula was chosen for Hilltop Station's placement precisely because the neck could be sealed in a containment emergency. On day 3 of the Cascade, the neck WAS sealed, by the Warden Garrison following orders from a chain of command that went silent by day 6. The seal is still active. Aether Group broadcasts tell survivors the seal will open "soon."

## Timeline of the collapse

### [COMMIT] Day-by-day skeleton (writers reference this)

Writers producing holotapes, terminal entries, journal fragments, and NPC dialog should calibrate their timestamps against this skeleton. Any document dated "day 14" must be consistent with day-14 conditions on the peninsula.

- **Day 1:** Containment breach at Hilltop Station. Officially, a "minor research incident." Three hospitalizations in Mercy.
- **Day 3:** Peninsula neck sealed by Warden Garrison under direct order from national command. Communications inside/outside the peninsula throttled. Civilians told it's a "precautionary quarantine."
- **Day 6:** National chain of command goes silent. Warden Garrison command structure holds but receives no external orders.
- **Day 8:** First confirmed cryptid sighting in Coalton (cross-ref "The Coalton Horror" cryptid lore). Cascade 2 begins.
- **Day 11:** Power grid fails in Coalton and the inland villages. Mercy and Old Port retain power via local generation.
- **Day 14:** Aether Group first identified. Hilltop Station staff who survived the breach have re-branded themselves as a "reconstruction authority." Their first broadcast goes out.
- **Day 17:** Old Mercy Hospital falls. Approximately 1,200 staff and patients. Cross-ref "Old Mercy Hospital" quest line.
- **Day 19:** Global financial collapse (irrelevant to peninsula survivors but canonically documented).
- **Day 22:** Warden Garrison refuses an Aether Group demand to surrender command of the Hilltop perimeter. The two factions are now in cold conflict.
- **Day 28:** Firebombing of the inland villages begins. Aether Group claims responsibility "as necessary containment." The Carrion faction forms in response, initially as a self-defense cooperative of displaced villagers.
- **Day 31:** The Aether-Warden skirmish at the Hilltop perimeter. Neither side openly admits it happened. Both sides lost heavily. A player who finds the battlefield can piece it together.
- **Day 37:** The Settlers faction forms. A group of refugees from the firebombed villages stops running and establishes the settlement later known as **Settler's Hold**.
- **Day 41:** First sermons of The Faithful recorded. Their founder, Pastor **Elias Roe**, had been a pre-Cascade preacher; his theology crystallizes in response to the cryptid sightings.
- **Day 44:** The Wayward caravans begin formal operation. Pre-Cascade trucker and nomad networks organize under a shared code of conduct.
- **Day 47:** Last non-Aether radio transmission off-peninsula. Whether the outside world still exists is unknown from inside.

Between day 47 and "eleven months ago" (day of cryo-wakeup): the peninsula settles into the present-day equilibrium. Infected populations stabilize in abandoned cities. Cryptid populations stabilize in specific biomes. Factions solidify territory. The Network forms as an information economy around the scraps.

## Aether Group

### [COMMIT] Origin

Aether Group is the **rebranded survivors of Hilltop Station's leadership and security staff**, plus a subset of the researchers who did not die in the initial breach. On day 14 they declared themselves a "reconstruction authority" and seized the station's broadcast infrastructure. Their founding members include:

- **Director Sabine Voss** (pre-Cascade: Hilltop Station director of operations). Still alive. Late-50s, pragmatic, competent, ruthless, and has convinced herself that Aether Group's survival IS humanity's survival and that all means are therefore justified. Not a cartoon villain.
- **Dr. Raymond Lao** (pre-Cascade: SERAPH-3 lead researcher). Still alive. Late-60s, in poor health, bears the real technical knowledge of what SERAPH-3 was. Lao is the single most important NPC for the lore late-game arc; a player who reaches him and interrogates him fully learns almost everything.
- **Captain Eliana Durand** (pre-Cascade: Hilltop security chief). Still alive. Early-40s, the operational face of Aether Group's enforcement arm, commands the Group's soldiers.
- Roughly forty surviving Hilltop staff, plus recruited post-Cascade converts.

### [COMMIT] Agenda

Publicly, Aether Group says it is coordinating peninsula reconstruction, providing medical aid, and working toward opening the neck seal. Privately, their actual objectives are:

1. **Survival of Aether Group as an institution.** They have the most remaining pre-Cascade infrastructure, the most remaining technical knowledge, and the most to lose.
2. **Containment of SERAPH-3 evidence.** Any document, holotape, survivor, or piece of equipment that could prove Aether Group's predecessors engineered the pathogen must be suppressed. This drives most of their hostile operations. The player will find Aether Group soldiers burning archives, hunting specific named individuals, and shooting "looters" who happened to pick up the wrong piece of paper.
3. **Re-establishment of contact with the outside.** Voss believes the outside world may still exist and that Aether Group's position depends on being the peninsula's representative when contact is re-made. Recovery of working satellite uplink gear is their long-term technical goal.
4. **Cryptid research.** Dr. Lao is studying the mutagenic tail. Some of the research is humanitarian. Some of it is weaponization.

### [COMMIT] Present-day operation

Aether Group holds Hilltop Station itself, a secondary base at the pre-Cascade airport, and a network of broadcast relays. They patrol in squads of four-to-eight soldiers in heavy pre-Cascade-military gear. Their civilian "processing" outposts offer food, basic medical care, and radio news in exchange for ID registration and resource levies. Most civilians who enter a processing outpost do come back out; a small percentage do not, and the ones who don't are usually the ones who came in with something Voss wanted.

Aether Group is a late-game faction. Early- and mid-game players interact with them through radio broadcasts and distant patrols. A main-quest arc brings the player to direct confrontation.

### [UNDECIDED] Is Aether Group redeemable?

Two late-game branches:

- **A) Aether Group is redeemable.** Voss can be confronted with evidence, fractured from her hardliners, and converted into a genuine reconstruction authority. The peninsula enters a "rebuilding" epilogue.
- **B) Aether Group is irredeemable.** Voss' ideology cannot bend. The player either takes Aether Group apart (with one of the other factions as an ally) or joins them and perpetuates the cycle.

**Recommendation: both branches ship.** Player choice + faction alignment + discovered evidence determine which is available. This is cross-referenced in PLAN-Quests-Storyline.md late-game arc.

## The cryptids

### [COMMIT] Actual origin

As established in Cascade 2: cryptids are humans (and in rare cases, animals) who survived SERAPH-3 infection and underwent a retroviral mutagenic tail. The retrovirus contained splice material of multiple origins:

- Amphibian regenerative genes (contributed "The Sodden," swamp cryptid with limb regrowth)
- Cephalopod chromatophore DNA (contributed "The Pale Woman," urban cryptid with active camouflage)
- Bat echolocation and auditory cortex material (contributed "The Caller," forest cryptid that hunts by sound, cross-ref Infected behavior)
- Insect exoskeleton keratin pathways (contributed "The Husks")
- And one strand of material whose origin is NOT on any pre-Cascade record (contributed "The Mother," whose physiology does not match any known terrestrial organism). This is a late-game mystery. The recommended canonical answer is that the splice material was sourced from a pre-Cascade xenoarchaeological sample Hilltop Station received from a classified military dig site. This is a DEFER until writers have budget to build it out.

Each named cryptid has a specific human-origin identity. The Coalton Horror was a plant foreman named Bennett Wren. The Pale Woman was a pediatric nurse at Old Mercy named Claire Sandow. A player who chases cryptid lore can find their pre-Cascade photographs, IDs, homes. Some players will learn the cryptid they just killed was a specific human.

**This is the game's emotional core.** The cryptids are not monsters. They are the sick, and they used to be people, and some of them still recognize names or faces, and the player is sometimes the one who kills them.

### [COMMIT] In-world accounts

- **Aether Group:** "Cryptids are a consequence of prolonged untreated infection. Avoid, do not engage, report sightings." They know exactly what cryptids are. They are covering it up.
- **The Faithful:** "Cryptids are angels of judgment sent to cleanse the unworthy. Do not look upon them. Do not speak their names." A convenient theology for survivors who have seen their neighbors transform.
- **The Mothfolk:** Cryptids are **the next stage of humanity**. The Mothfolk believe the mutagenic tail is evolutionary and that the correct response is to invite it, by exposing themselves to SERAPH-3 intentionally. Some Mothfolk are already mid-transformation. Their leader, **the Moth Priestess**, is a former researcher from Hilltop who defected on day 22 and has been intentionally infecting herself ever since. She is not yet a cryptid. She is also not yet human. She is the third-most-important lore NPC after Lao and Voss.
- **The Carrion:** "Monsters." No further theory. The Carrion hunt cryptids for meat and for trophies.

## The Infected

### [COMMIT] Origin and pathogen pathway

The Infected are the SERAPH-3 primary-effect survivors. Most are Stage-1 (aggressive, sound-sensitive, but physically normal). A subset progress to Stage-2 (physical changes, coordinated pack behavior, some primitive tool use). Stage-3 is the cryptid mutation. Stage-0 is the occasional full-survivor: a person who got sick, got better, and is neurologically mostly themselves with minor lingering effects (decreased long-term memory, tinnitus, heightened sound sensitivity). Stage-0 survivors are rare, stigmatized, and are an NPC demographic (some player-friendly traders are Stage-0).

The Infected are not hostile to each other within a cluster. They ARE sensitive to sound. They DO respond to pheromone/scent cues from cryptids (specifically, cryptids can summon nearby Infected). These details are already in PLAN-Infected-AI.md; this plan just anchors them to the lore.

### [COMMIT] Current state

Eleven months in, the Infected population has stabilized. New infections are rare (SERAPH-3 requires close respiratory contact, and most survivors have learned precautions). Existing Infected have aged; some have died to starvation, fights, cryptids, or Stage-2/3 progression. The Infected population is treated by the world as "weather" not "news," which is correct, and is exactly the mechanic PLAN-Infected-AI.md encodes.

## Faction histories

This section fleshes out each NPC faction's origin beyond the one-line descriptions in PLAN-Factions-Squads.md.

### [COMMIT] The Settlers

**Origin:** Day 37. A group of ~200 refugees from the firebombed inland villages stopped running and began building at what is now Settler's Hold. Their founder, **Maren Okaforo**, was a pre-Cascade agricultural cooperative organizer. She is still alive, in her early 60s, and is the Settlers' de facto leader.

**Ideology:** Rebuild. Farm. Take in who you can. Trade fairly. Stay armed but don't pick fights. The Settlers are the "good faction" in the basic sense of the word, but they have hard edges: they turn away cryptid-adjacent refugees, they have killed Carrion raiders who attacked them, and they levy a food tax on passing Wayward caravans.

**Relationship to lore:** The Settlers don't know the full Cascade truth. They suspect Aether Group is "not quite what they say" but have no evidence.

### [COMMIT] The Wardens

**Origin:** Pre-Cascade. The Wardens are the remnant of the peninsula's military Warden Garrison, the unit that sealed the neck on day 3 and held command through day 47. Their current leader, **Colonel Thomas Prewitt**, is the surviving ranking officer.

**Ideology:** Order, discipline, the chain of command survives even if the command structure above us doesn't. The Wardens have a grudge against Aether Group dating back to the day-31 skirmish and another against the Carrion dating back to several documented atrocities. They distrust scientists (correctly, given Hilltop's role) but cannot say so publicly because a significant fraction of their medical knowledge came from Hilltop before day 22.

**Relationship to lore:** The Wardens know MORE than the Settlers. Colonel Prewitt was on the peninsula command chain when Hilltop's breach was first reported. He has suppressed the detail from his own unit to maintain discipline. A player who gains Prewitt's trust can learn this.

### [COMMIT] The Wayward

**Origin:** Day 44. Formed from pre-Cascade trucker networks and Romani-adjacent nomad communities that were already mobile and organized when the Cascade began. Their current leader, **Dabi Ruse**, is a third-generation caravan master.

**Ideology:** Movement is safety. Trade is diplomacy. Neutrality is earned. The Wayward do not take sides in faction wars. They will trade with Aether Group, Carrion, and Faithful equally (at different prices, charged in different currencies). They protect their routes fiercely and retaliate against anyone who ambushes a caravan.

**Relationship to lore:** The Wayward are the KEEPERS of inter-faction information. Dabi Ruse knows who knows what, and can be bribed or traded with to connect a player to specific other NPCs. This makes the Wayward a gameplay-critical faction disproportionate to their military strength.

### [COMMIT] The Faithful

**Origin:** Day 41. Pastor **Elias Roe**, a pre-Cascade preacher with a modest podcast following, gathered ~80 survivors at what is now the Faithful compound (a pre-Cascade church conference center in the coastal region). His theology crystallized in response to the cryptid sightings and the firebombings (which he framed as purifying fire).

**Ideology:** The Cascade is divine judgment. Survivors are being tested. Cryptids are angels of wrath; the Infected are the damned. Cleansing the world is holy work. The Faithful are not uniformly violent, but their militant arm, the **Purifiers**, hunt cryptids and "tainted" humans (Stage-0 survivors, anyone showing mutation).

**Relationship to lore:** The Faithful do not want to know the truth. Pastor Roe in particular would be destabilized by evidence that the Cascade was man-made, and avoids conversations that might go there. A player who confronts him with the truth gets one of the game's most interesting dialog branches: Roe either breaks, denies, or (rare path) accepts and dismantles his own church.

### [COMMIT] The Carrion

**Origin:** Day 28, in direct response to the Aether Group firebombing of the inland villages. Initially a self-defense cooperative of displaced villagers. Devolved into raider clans over the following months as resource scarcity and vendetta culture hardened. The current "Carrion Kings" are a rotating handful of warlords; the name is the title, not a person.

**Ideology:** The strong take. The weak work. Aether Group is the enemy. Everyone else is a resource or an obstacle. The Carrion are not monolithic; some bands are almost Wayward-like in their codes of conduct, others are predatory in every way. Players encounter both.

**Relationship to lore:** The Carrion know "someone caused this" and their hatred of Aether Group is sharper than their hatred of the Wardens. A few Carrion veterans who were present on day 28 know specific details that would be valuable to the player.

### [COMMIT] The Network

**Origin:** Gradually, days 60-120. Not a faction in the military sense; more of a cooperative of information brokers who found each other through Wayward routes. Their unofficial head is a figure called only **"the Archivist,"** whose identity is unknown to most Network members themselves. (Canonically, the Archivist is a pre-Cascade investigative journalist named **Ines Martel** who has spent eleven months systematically collecting and cross-referencing every piece of lore on the peninsula. She is the best-informed person on the peninsula and Dr. Lao's only competition for total knowledge.)

**Ideology:** Information is the currency that survives when money dies. The Network trades in secrets, maps, names, schedules, passwords. They are not loyal to any faction; they sell to all of them.

**Relationship to lore:** The Network is the lore quest-giver faction. A player building a relationship with the Archivist unlocks progressively deeper lore quests, eventually culminating in the peninsula-history meta-quest that ties together every other plot thread.

### [COMMIT] The Mothfolk

**Origin:** Day 60ish, emergent. Formed around the Moth Priestess (see Cryptids section) and her initial followers (Hilltop defectors and cryptid-encounter survivors who interpreted their survival as communion). The Mothfolk have no pre-Cascade equivalent; they are a new culture.

**Ideology:** The mutagenic tail is evolution. Humans are being given a gift. The cryptids are ancestors-to-be. Self-infection is a sacrament. Violence against cryptids is a sacrilege. The Mothfolk are physically dangerous (many are mid-mutation) and psychologically difficult to negotiate with.

**Relationship to lore:** The Mothfolk know things Aether Group wants suppressed, because the Moth Priestess was at Hilltop on day 22. A player who gains her trust (difficult, requires specific choices) learns the Hilltop breach story from an insider's perspective.

## Lore artifact catalog (seeds for Quests system)

This is the writer's menu. Every entry here is a concrete piece of lore content that the Quests system can reference. PLAN-Quests-Storyline.md promises ~200 holotapes; this catalog seeds them.

### Holotape seeds (target: 200 total, seeded below in priorities)

**[COMMIT] Priority tier 1 (must exist for main questline):**

- **HT-Cascade-Helvetia-1/2/3** - Dr. Helvetia's account of the leak. Each tape raises stakes. Tape 3 names the consortium.
- **HT-Hilltop-Voss-Internal-1** - Voss' recorded staff briefing post-breach. Reveals Aether Group's early strategy.
- **HT-Hilltop-Lao-Research-1/2** - Dr. Lao's voice notes on the retrovirus and the mutagenic tail. Confidential tone, clinical language.
- **HT-Peninsula-Seal-Prewitt-1** - Colonel Prewitt's standing order on day 3 to seal the neck. Explains the peninsula's isolation.
- **HT-Mercy-Hospital-Fall-1/2** - A nurse's sequential voice-notes during the day-17 hospital collapse. Emotional, harrowing, ships with the Old Mercy Hospital landmark.

**[COMMIT] Priority tier 2 (main faction arcs):**

- 3-5 holotapes per faction leader (origin story, key decision moments, private doubts)
- 2-3 holotapes per cryptid origin (pre-transformation life)
- 4-6 holotapes for day-28 firebombing survivors (Carrion origin)

**[LIKELY] Priority tier 3 (ambient, texture):**

- Pre-Cascade civilian holotapes: birthday messages, arguments, voicemails, business calls, podcast episodes, radio shows. ~80-120 of these. Zero plot weight, maximum texture. Writer's playground.

### Document seeds

- Pre-Cascade corporate documents (Hilltop Station letterhead, funding proposals with redacted names)
- Pre-Cascade military orders (peninsula quarantine doctrine, warden garrison roster)
- Post-Cascade faction documents (Settlers crop records, Wayward trade ledgers, Faithful sermon notes, Network coded dispatches)
- Medical records (hospital charts, pharmacy logs, research subject files - especially sensitive at Hilltop)
- Personal documents (diaries, letters, drawings, children's homework)

### Terminal entries

- Aether Group internal terminals: patrol schedules, processing center quotas, suppressed document lists (huge late-game reveal)
- Warden command terminals: tactical assessments of the peninsula, honest estimates of Aether Group strength
- Pre-Cascade civilian terminals: personal computers, library archives, school systems (for emotional texture and occasional relevant detail)
- Hilltop research terminals: the single densest lore resource in the game; late-game gated

### Environmental storytelling seeds

- The school where the teachers barricaded the classroom and all died with the kids (Settlers territory)
- The pharmacy whose owner dispensed a month of free antibiotics before locking up and shooting himself
- The cryo-shelter the player wakes up in: five other pods. Four are empty. One is still occupied. That player's choice whether to open it.
- The suburb where every house has its porch light on eleven months later because the grid hasn't gone out
- The hospital ward still playing a looping soothing-music track
- The radio tower whose dead DJ is still in the booth, headphones on, a hundred days after starvation

Each of these is a scene a player can walk into and read without any NPC or holotape being necessary. The environment tells it. This is the mode Quests-Storyline.md's principle #1 asks for.

## Writer's reference: style notes

- **Holotapes should sound like humans, not exposition devices.** Interruptions, ambient noise, people saying the wrong name, pauses, "sorry I'm recording this at work."
- **No omniscient narrators.** Every document has an author and every author has limits.
- **Pre-Cascade voice is optimistic in a way the player will find heartbreaking.** A birthday voicemail for a kid who didn't make it. A wedding invitation in a burned house.
- **Post-Cascade voice is exhausted, not theatrical.** Survivors don't monologue.
- **Aether Group broadcasts are smooth, professional, and deeply wrong in ways you can hear if you listen.** Trained voice talent reading sentences written by a committee that does not trust the audience.
- **The Faithful's rhetoric escalates on a clear gradient.** Early sermons are recognizably Christian and grieving. Late sermons are terrifying.
- **The Mothfolk do not explain themselves in any recorded medium.** Their lore is entirely oral, witnessed, or environmental. A player who wants Mothfolk truth has to go get it in person.
