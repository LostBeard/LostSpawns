# Lost Spawns: Cryptid Biology & Encounter Design

## Status Legend
- **[COMMIT]** settled design decisions
- **[LIKELY]** strong preference, expect to commit
- **[UNDECIDED]** open
- **[DEFER]** post-1.0
- **[REJECT]** explicitly not doing

---

## Premise

PLAN-Lore-History established the Cascade: SERAPH-3 pathogen spliced human and animal DNA through a retroviral mutagenic tail, creating hybrid creatures. Four cascades over 47 days. Day 0 releases the pathogen. Day 12 cryptids manifest. Day 47 networks of mature creatures dominate certain regions.

This plan defines what those creatures are at the species level. Not "zombies" - Lost Spawns is explicitly not a zombie game. The antagonists are mutated living things with continuous biology, territorial behavior, hunger and fear and even curiosity. Some are bipedal; some are not. Some retain enough human cognition to be approachable. Most do not.

The design rule: **every cryptid is a recognizable mammalian/avian/reptilian base plus a "wrong" addition.** A wolf-silhouette that runs too low to the ground. A human that walks with elbows up. A bird that does not fly but sprints. This uncanniness is the horror layer. Not rot, not undeath - mutation, visibly alive, visibly wrong.

---

## Design Principles

### 1. Not Zombies

**[COMMIT]** Cryptids are alive. They breathe, bleed, eat, sleep, heal. They are not reanimated corpses. They were living things before the Cascade; they are living things now, just reconfigured.

**[COMMIT]** Cryptids do not "infect" other creatures by bite. SERAPH-3 is an aerosol pathogen delivered in the Cascade events themselves. Once the cascades end, the pathogen is bound in existing cryptid biology and can no longer infect fresh hosts. A bite from a cryptid does damage and carries ordinary infection risk (sepsis), but does not mutate the victim.

**[REJECT]** Zombie tropes: reanimated dead, bite-to-infect, headshot-to-kill, shuffling hordes, slow chase. None of these. Lost Spawns' cryptids are fast, smart-enough, scary, and bleed when shot.

### 2. Recognizable Silhouettes, Wrong Proportions

**[COMMIT]** Every cryptid species has a "base" animal the player can identify at a glance (wolf, bear, human, deer, hawk). The horror is in the mutation: extra limbs, wrong ratios, visible asymmetry, hybrid features from two species.

**[COMMIT]** The uncanniness is design-intentional. A deer-cryptid looks like a deer until it opens its mouth wider than any deer should, or until it stands on two legs.

### 3. Territorial Not Horde

**[COMMIT]** Cryptids claim territory. Some are solitary ambush predators; some run in small packs; some guard specific structures (the Cascade-originated networks around Hilltop Station). Very few mass in hordes, and those that do are rare dynamic events, not a constant threat.

**[COMMIT]** Territory = gameplay information. Learning where the Marrow-Elk herd roams, or which cave system the Spine-Wolves den in, is a viable survival strategy. Map knowledge beats raw firepower.

### 4. Ecology, Not Spawn Counts

**[COMMIT]** Cryptids have a food web. They eat ordinary wildlife. Populations fluctuate based on available prey. Over-hunting an area's deer means fewer wolves and fewer Spine-Wolves. Ecology-driven density.

**[LIKELY]** Cryptids can scavenge. Dead bodies (human or animal) attract nearby cryptids. Players can use this: drop a corpse to lure a cryptid out of a territory, or to attract one cryptid so you can slip past another.

### 5. Knowable, Beatable, Memorable

**[COMMIT]** Each species has a consistent vulnerability, a consistent attack pattern, a consistent sound. Players learn them over time. A veteran player reads a footprint and knows what approaches.

**[COMMIT]** Every cryptid can be killed with ordinary firearms or melee. Nothing is bullet-immune. Some are extremely hard (bullet-sponge + fast), but headshots, limb shots, and explosive damage work on all.

## Species Catalog

### Tier 1: Common (found across the peninsula)

#### Spine-Wolf (Canis alpha-altered)
- **Base:** wolf
- **Wrong:** second spine along the flank, partial extra ribcage. Runs on four legs but can briefly stand on hind legs when threatening. Gray fur turning patchy at mutation sites.
- **Behavior:** pack of 3-6, territorial, actively hunts. Attacks from multiple angles. Howls to coordinate pack.
- **Vulnerability:** double spine is structurally weaker than a normal wolf's single spine - a well-placed rifle shot mid-back can one-hit a Spine-Wolf that no shot can take a normal wolf.
- **Drops:** wolf pelt (mutated, worth less than clean wolf), cryptid marrow (crafting ingredient), occasional SERAPH-3 trace samples (research value).
- **Sound:** wolf howl with a faint second voice underneath - harmony an octave off.

#### Marrow-Elk (Cervus meta-altered)
- **Base:** elk / deer
- **Wrong:** antlers grown from shoulders as well as head. Hooves split into three claws. Eyes red-shot from hypermetabolism.
- **Behavior:** solitary bulls defend territory aggressively. Cows move in small herds. Do not chase far - attack once and retreat. Can impale with shoulder-antlers during charge.
- **Vulnerability:** Marrow-Elk bones are dense but brittle. Explosives shatter them; small-caliber rifles take many shots. Aim for the shoulder-antler growths - disrupts the charge attack.
- **Drops:** marrow-elk venison (potentially SERAPH-3-tainted - see PLAN-Animal-Wildlife for tainted meat rules), large antler sections (crafting + trade).
- **Sound:** deep bellow that vibrates the ground, audible from 200m+.

#### Scavver (Corvus meta-altered)
- **Base:** crow/raven
- **Wrong:** wingspan 2x normal. Beak hooked and oversized. Talons human-fingered.
- **Behavior:** flocks of 5-15. Harass survivors near corpses. Steal small items (ammo, medical supplies) if backpack is open and player is still. Not a lethal threat individually; a flock can blind/panic you.
- **Vulnerability:** shot gun or any area weapon. Individual Scavvers drop in one bullet. The flock is a swarm threat, not a tank threat.
- **Drops:** Scavver feathers (fletching material), stolen trinkets (ammo, pills, small items) if you kill one carrying.
- **Sound:** unsettling caw harmonic - multiple voices layered.

#### Huskling (Homo sapiens-altered, early stage)
- **Base:** human
- **Wrong:** skin pale to the point of translucent. Joints overextended (elbows bend wrong way, gait shambling). Eyes whitish-gray, cataract-like but alert. Still wear clothes from pre-Cascade; clothes rotting, skin showing through.
- **Behavior:** solitary or pairs. Low aggression, high curiosity. Approach players slowly. Can be non-violent if the player does not threaten. If attacked, retaliate frantically with hand-to-hand.
- **Vulnerability:** still fundamentally human biology - headshots kill instantly; center-mass kills with ordinary rounds. Fragile.
- **Drops:** rotting civilian gear (tattered clothes, personal effects). Rarely, a scrap of holotape or paper document from the Cascade.
- **Sound:** almost-human moaning, occasional intelligible words. Rarely: whole sentences of pre-Cascade memories that loop.

**[LIKELY]** Husklings are the "moral gray zone" cryptid. Killing one does not feel good. Some players will try to leave them alone. This is intentional - it is the kind of texture DayZ never had.

### Tier 2: Uncommon (specific biomes or circumstances)

#### Bone-Bear (Ursus cascade-3)
- **Base:** brown/black bear
- **Wrong:** partial external skeleton - rib bones grown through skin into armor plates. Claws longer than forearm. Face elongated, bear-skull visible through skin.
- **Behavior:** solitary apex predator. Does not hunt; ambushes. Sleeps in caves during the day, roams at night.
- **Vulnerability:** armor plates turn most rifle rounds. Gaps between plates (joints, neck, belly) are kill zones. Armor-piercing ammunition, explosives, or a well-placed melee hit behind the neck.
- **Drops:** bone plate (crafting: armor material), massive pelt, bear claws (melee weapon crafting), high-value meat if uncontaminated.
- **Sound:** low rumble + wet breathing. Roar audible 500m+.

#### Glass-Spider (arachnid cascade-3)
- **Base:** large wolf spider / tarantula
- **Wrong:** size of a german shepherd. Translucent chitin showing internal organs working. Eight limbs, each with tactile pad.
- **Behavior:** builds tunnel nests in ruins, cellars, old warehouses. Ambushes from ceiling or floor. Not pack animals; multiple can claim the same building.
- **Vulnerability:** fire is extremely effective - the translucent chitin is flammable. Shotgun slugs or any high-damage shot kill fast. Fragile overall.
- **Drops:** silk-glass (crafting material, lightweight armor), venom sacs (toxin extraction for crafting or trade).
- **Sound:** clicking chitin-on-chitin, silent otherwise. You feel them before you hear them (webs vibrating).

#### Scrap-Hawk (raptor cascade-3)
- **Base:** hawk / osprey
- **Wrong:** wingspan 3m+. Metal fragments embedded in feathers (absorbed during pre-Cascade industrial mutations). Carry weight capacity far exceeds normal raptors.
- **Behavior:** solitary aerial hunter. Patrols open terrain. Picks up small prey (rabbits, dogs) and drops them from height. Reports say they have carried adult humans short distances. Mostly: avoids dense forest.
- **Vulnerability:** still a bird - a well-placed rifle shot brings them down. Hard to hit a moving flying target; high-powered scoped weapons needed. Ground-based melee: irrelevant.
- **Drops:** enormous feathers (fletching + decoration), talons (melee weapon crafting), occasionally the corpse includes whatever prey they were carrying (unexpected loot).
- **Sound:** metallic screech. Audible before visible.

#### Crawler (Homo cascade-3, late stage)
- **Base:** human (further mutated than Huskling)
- **Wrong:** limbs elongated - arms nearly as long as the body. Walks on all fours with elbows up. Oversized jaws. Often naked, or wearing the shredded remains of clothes.
- **Behavior:** pack of 2-5. Aggressive. Climb walls, drop from ceilings. Fast but low stamina - can be outrun over distance.
- **Vulnerability:** human cardiovascular - headshots, heart shots. Fragile for their size. Low stamina means a good sprinter can outrun them.
- **Drops:** mutated human tissue (research value, morally unsettling), clothing scraps.
- **Sound:** panting, scratching of bone-hard fingernails on surfaces. Rarely vocalize, but when they do: hoarse scream.

### Tier 3: Rare (specific spawns, high threat)

#### Hive-Queen (swarm controller)
- **Base:** ??? (mass of tissue, seemingly no single base species)
- **Wrong:** immobile. 3+ meters across. Cradles smaller Crawlers that emerge to defend. Appears to direct pack behavior of nearby cryptids. Found deep in the Cascade networks.
- **Behavior:** does not move. Emits a subsonic pulse that modifies nearby cryptid behavior - makes packs cohere, coordinates attacks, draws reinforcements.
- **Vulnerability:** sustained heavy damage. Explosives. Flamethrower if we add one. Killing a Hive-Queen calms nearby cryptids for hours.
- **Drops:** major research material (SERAPH-3 concentrated samples - high trade value with Wardens). Unique artifacts (pre-Cascade Aether Group equipment often embedded in the tissue).
- **Sound:** subsonic pulse you feel more than hear. Cryptids near a Hive-Queen sound coordinated.
- **Encounter density:** ~1-5 per map. Discoverable via network signs (increased cryptid density, unusual coordination).

#### Shadelark (Homo cascade-3, alpha stage)
- **Base:** human (fully mutated, retains cognition)
- **Wrong:** tall, thin, skin matte-black, eyes glowing faint white. Moves with predator fluidity. Can speak in full sentences but often chooses not to.
- **Behavior:** extremely rare. Solitary. Observed leading Crawler packs or Huskling groups. Will parley with players under specific conditions - typically demanding the player surrender an item, tell a truth, or perform a task. If the player refuses, it does not necessarily attack; it may retreat and return reinforced.
- **Vulnerability:** still human, still killable. Fast and dangerous in melee; most lethal to players who approach it as a monster rather than a person. Keeps distance, prefers ambush from shadow.
- **Drops:** pre-Cascade human artifacts (intact holotapes, sometimes personal photographs). A defeated Shadelark drops unique items; a parleyed-with Shadelark may give them willingly in exchange.
- **Sound:** low-register voice, speaks English fluently. Sometimes sings pre-Cascade songs fragmentally.
- **Encounter density:** ~3-5 per map.

**[LIKELY]** Shadelarks are the single most distinctive cryptid encounter. They exist specifically to subvert the "kill everything that looks wrong" reflex. Veteran players will learn to parley. New players who shoot on sight will face consequences (reinforcements, reputation hit with the Wayward faction who view Shadelarks as spirits).

**[UNDECIDED]** Do we let a Shadelark become an ally? Temporarily or permanently? Intense design decision. Feel-check this deep in playtesting before committing.

#### Walking Tower (mass-fusion cascade-3)
- **Base:** probably horse or cow, fused with human remains
- **Wrong:** 4m tall, 6 legs, mass of bodies at the top. Moves slowly. Rare in open terrain.
- **Behavior:** wanders. Does not actively hunt; any creature (player, cryptid) it encounters is attacked. A mobile disaster.
- **Vulnerability:** massive sustained damage. Anti-materiel weapons (a .50 cal if we have it), explosives, vehicle-mounted weapons. A lone player cannot kill one with ordinary gear.
- **Drops:** many weapons/items embedded in fused-body mass. Rare pre-Cascade military gear.
- **Sound:** chorus of moans and whimpers from fused humans. Horrifying and pitiable.
- **Encounter density:** ~1 per map. A Walking Tower is a world event (PLAN-Dynamic-World-Events).

## Cryptid Ecology

### Population Dynamics

**[LIKELY]** Each chunk tracks a "cryptid load": roughly how many of each species are alive in that chunk. Load responds to:
- Prey availability (more deer = more Marrow-Elk)
- Recent player kills (depletes, repopulates over days)
- Proximity to Hive-Queen (increases load, coordinated behavior)
- Season (winter reduces numbers, spring increases)
- Weather (see PLAN-Weather for how storms affect behavior)

**[LIKELY]** Cryptids migrate - a pack that runs out of food moves to a new chunk. Players can track this, hunt ahead of the migration, or drive a pack toward a rival faction.

### Nests and Territories

**[LIKELY]** Each species has territorial behavior:
- Spine-Wolves: den in caves or abandoned buildings. Can be destroyed/cleared to reduce local population.
- Marrow-Elk: territorial bull holds ~1 km². Killing the bull scatters the cows; a new bull eventually claims the territory.
- Bone-Bear: cave dens in elevated terrain. Rare, heavily defended loot.
- Glass-Spider: multi-generational tunnel networks. Clearing one is hours of work.
- Crawler: roost in tall structures (upper floors of ruined buildings, attics). Sleep during day, hunt at night.
- Scavver: communal roosts on rooftops, high ledges.

**[LIKELY]** Destroying a nest is satisfying (XP + loot + temporary regional calm) but fills out over time. The world does not become "cleared." It rebalances.

### Food Web

**[LIKELY]** Cryptids eat:
- Ordinary wildlife (deer, rabbits, fish)
- Other cryptids (larger eat smaller - Bone-Bears eat Spine-Wolves, etc.)
- Human corpses
- Occasionally each other's offspring (intra-species competition)

**[LIKELY]** If a player wipes out ordinary wildlife in a region, cryptid populations follow, creating either: a lull (briefly safe) or cannibalistic cryptid-on-cryptid competition (more intra-cryptid sounds, maybe safer for the player paradoxically).

## Encounter Design

### Tell-Tale Signs

Before you see a cryptid, you should be able to detect them. The world gives you warnings:

**[LIKELY]** Environmental cues:
- **Footprints** in mud/snow - species-identifiable with practice
- **Scratch marks** on trees or walls - claws, height tells you species
- **Carcasses** - fresh kill = cryptid nearby; bones = old territory
- **Scent markers** - piles of scat, territorial markings (cryptids with a sense of "home")
- **Silence** of normal wildlife - birds stop calling when a predator is near
- **Smell** - unique per species (see PLAN-Audio-Design for audio cues; visual smell indicator in HUD TBD)

**[LIKELY]** Audio cues:
- Spine-Wolf howl in the distance = ~500m away, approaching or holding
- Marrow-Elk bellow = territorial warning, turn back or fight
- Scavver flock caw = corpse nearby, risk/reward
- Subsonic Hive-Queen pulse = you are within a network, high danger

### Combat Patterns

**[COMMIT]** Every cryptid has a tell before it attacks. A Spine-Wolf lowers its head and snarls. A Marrow-Elk paws the ground. A Crawler raises its arms. Reading these is a skill.

**[COMMIT]** Combat should never feel like "press fire until it dies." Enemies have patterns. Players who learn the patterns survive.

**[LIKELY]** Distinct combat feel per species:
- Spine-Wolves: hit-and-run, coordinate with pack
- Marrow-Elk: single devastating charge, then retreat
- Bone-Bear: ignores most damage, slow but lethal in grapple
- Crawler: fast, high damage, low HP - reward accurate shooting
- Husklings: pathetic melee, easy to kill, morally expensive
- Shadelark: avoids open combat, sets ambushes, fast when pressed

### Loot Philosophy

**[COMMIT]** Killing a cryptid drops materials useful for:
- Crafting (bones, pelts, teeth, venom, silk-glass)
- Cooking (meat - sometimes tainted)
- Trading (unique drops valued by specific factions)
- Research progression (SERAPH-3 samples for Wardens)
- Narrative artifacts (rare drops from named cryptids)

**[COMMIT]** No cryptid drops human-tier loot directly (e.g. functioning firearms). They drop what they ARE - biological material. Human gear on cryptids is decorative/environmental only; if a Huskling is wearing a pre-Cascade uniform, the uniform is rotted beyond use.

## Adaptive Difficulty

**[UNDECIDED]** Cryptid density and aggression could respond to player skill/equipment:
- A player in full military gear encounters more Bone-Bears and Crawlers
- A player naked and unarmed encounters more Husklings and fewer aggressive species
- Could this be "rubber-banding" in a bad way? Or does it make the world feel responsive?

Leaning: no explicit scaling. Density is region-based, not player-based. But players who are loud, who shoot into the air, who leave corpses around, draw more attention - this is simulation, not scaling.

## SERAPH-3 Mechanics

### Tainted Meat

**[COMMIT]** Cryptid meat has a chance to be SERAPH-3-tainted. Tainted meat causes the player's own SERAPH-3 exposure meter to rise. At high exposure: progressive symptoms (see PLAN-Medical for the disease model).

**[LIKELY]** Tainted vs. clean meat is not visually distinguishable from the corpse alone. Cooking kills the pathogen but does not tell you what was in the meat. A "testing kit" item (rare, Warden-made) can detect tainted meat before cooking.

**[LIKELY]** Tier 1 cryptids: 30% tainted chance
**[LIKELY]** Tier 2 cryptids: 70% tainted chance
**[LIKELY]** Tier 3 cryptids: 100% tainted chance (do not eat a Hive-Queen)

### Research Samples

**[COMMIT]** Killing a cryptid has a chance to drop a SERAPH-3 sample. Samples are tradable with the Wardens faction for research rewards (see PLAN-Factions-Squads).

**[LIKELY]** Samples degrade over time - research them or trade them quickly. Refrigeration (rare) preserves.

### Player Immunity

**[COMMIT]** Players cannot become cryptids. The player's character has innate SERAPH-3 resistance (the cryo-shelter backstory explains it - see PLAN-Lore-History). High exposure makes you SICK but does not turn you.

**[REJECT]** "Turn into a cryptid" as a player state. Breaks too many other systems (inventory, social, combat) and is narrative-counter to our "survivors of a post-Cascade world" framing. Maybe DEFER as a DLC-only game mode much later.

## Visual Design Guidance

**[COMMIT]** Cryptids render in the same voxel style as the rest of the world. No pre-rendered or imported high-poly models. Everything fits the aesthetic.

**[LIKELY]** Cryptid silhouettes are distinct enough to identify at distance. A Spine-Wolf vs. a regular wolf should be distinguishable at 50m through a scope.

**[LIKELY]** Animation uses procedural skeletal motion. The "wrongness" is partly in the gait - irregular timing, asymmetry between left and right, unexpected pauses. This is cheaper than hand-authored animations and scarier.

**[LIKELY]** Color palette: cryptids are bleached, pale, or matte-dark. Never saturated. Fits the game's muted palette. Blood is unnervingly pale as well (not bright red).

---

## Audio Design Guidance

**[LIKELY]** Each cryptid has a signature sound library: vocalizations, movement sounds, attack sounds, death sounds. Players will learn them.

**[LIKELY]** Spatial audio is critical. A Spine-Wolf's howl should localize. A Hive-Queen pulse should feel directional even if it is subsonic.

**[LIKELY]** Sound masking: rain and thunderstorms reduce player audibility of cryptids (and vice versa). See PLAN-Weather.

## Testing & Balance

**[COMMIT]** Each cryptid species has a telemetry target:
- Average kills per hour by veteran players
- Average deaths caused per hour
- Average reward vs. risk
- Player sentiment (is this encounter fun / tense / frustrating)

**[LIKELY]** Balance sweeps in beta. A cryptid that is universally feared but cheesed easily is a balance problem. One that is annoying but rewards nothing is a design problem.

**[COMMIT]** Playtest by TJ, Nikki, Aubs (age-appropriate - Husklings/Spine-Wolves OK for Aubs, Shadelarks probably OK, Crawlers maybe, Hive-Queen too much). Crew (crew = the SpawnDev team) playtests in swarm sessions.

## Open Questions

**[UNDECIDED]** Do we introduce new species in post-1.0 updates (new mutations emerging from lingering SERAPH-3)? Narrative space for it is there - the pathogen could have dormant effects. Could drive content cadence.

**[UNDECIDED]** Aquatic cryptids? The peninsula has coast. Fish-cryptids lurking underwater would be a DayZ-wasn't-there horror beat. Likely YES, coordinating with PLAN-Water-Rivers-Sea (not yet written).

**[UNDECIDED]** Flying cryptids beyond Scrap-Hawk? Bat-based? Insect-based? Scale impacts Scrap-Hawk's uniqueness.

**[UNDECIDED]** Multiplayer cryptid encounters - do cryptids scale with number of players in the area, or is a pack a pack regardless? Leaning: pack is a pack; multiple players beat them easier; that is correct.

**[UNDECIDED]** Can Aether Group (the antagonist faction) field cryptid units? Narratively the answer is YES (they created them). Gameplay-wise would be a late-game reveal. DEFER but keep space in the design.
